    What Is a Disposable Temporary Email?

    A disposable email is a free address you can use without registering and without handing over your real inbox. You paste it into a sign-up form, the website sends its confirmation message to it, and you read that message here. Nothing is tied to your name, and the address and everything it received are deleted automatically after 24 hours. It is the easiest way to avoid newsletters, marketing lists and data-broker mail from sites you only need once. Read the full explanation.

    How Tempamail.org works

    When you open the site, your browser generates a random address such as k3x9a1m0pq@tempamail.org and remembers it on your device so a page refresh does not change it. Every address at our domain is valid, so there is nothing to create or sign up for.

    When a website sends mail to that address, the message is delivered to our mail provider (Cloudflare Email Routing), handed to a small program that reads the sender, subject and body, and saved to a database under the recipient address. This page asks for new messages every ten seconds. After 24 hours the message is removed.

    What to know before you use it

    • It is public by design. Anyone who knows or guesses your exact address can read its inbox. The random part is long enough that guessing is impractical, but never use a temporary address for anything private.
    • It is receive-only. You cannot send mail from these addresses.
    • It is temporary. Messages are deleted after 24 hours, and there is no way to recover them. Do not use a temporary address for banking, account recovery, or any service you want to keep.
    • Some websites block disposable domains. If a site refuses the address, that is the site's choice. Please respect it rather than trying to get around it.
    • Remote images are blocked when you open a message here, so senders cannot use tracking pixels to see that you read it. Scripts are disabled as well.

    Good uses

    • Downloading a free guide or template that asks for an email first
    • Joining a forum or app you want to try once
    • Testing sign-up and email flows while building a website or app
    • Getting a verification code from a site you do not trust with your real address
